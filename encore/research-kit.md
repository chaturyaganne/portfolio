# Encore — usability research kit

Status: proposed study; no participants recruited or tested. Independent fictional ticketing concept, not affiliated with SeatGeek.

## Questions to answer

Can an organizer identify an option meeting every group's constraint? Can they explain an all-in per-person and group total? Do they distinguish step-free routes from wheelchair spaces? Is comparison useful, or does it add friction? What would they need to trust an actual venue's access information?

## Recruiting and consent

Proposed first round: five adults, including varied event-buying experience and, through appropriate voluntary recruiting, participants with access needs. Do not require medical disclosures. Explain the fictional scenario, that the design—not the participant—is being evaluated, and that they may stop at any time. Obtain separate consent for any recording. Collect only necessary observations and agree on storage/deletion. No real ticket purchase or financial details are required.

## Moderator opening

“Thank you for helping explore this early concept. The events and prices are fictional. We're testing whether the design makes sense, not your ability. Please think aloud if comfortable. I may wait before answering questions so I can see how the design communicates. Nothing you do will buy or reserve tickets.”

## Tasks (do not reveal the expected control sequence)

1. You're planning a night at Midnight Echo with three friends. Your group needs four adjacent seats, a clear view, a step-free route, and a maximum of $100 each including fees. Find an option that meets those needs.
2. Find another suitable option and compare it with your first choice. Explain which you prefer and why.
3. Tell me what each person would pay and the total for the group. What, if anything, could change before a real purchase?
4. Change the budget to $40 per person. What would you do next? Recover without unintentionally changing group size.
5. Prepare a plan for the group. Explain whether the plan holds seats, purchases tickets, or confirms wheelchair space. Copy the plan only if comfortable using your clipboard.

## Follow-up prompts

- What did you expect to happen there?
- Which detail do you trust most? Which would you want to verify?
- What does “step-free route” mean to you? Would that be enough to choose a wheelchair space?
- Where did you feel you were doing extra work?
- On a 1–7 scale, how easy or difficult was this task? Why?

Avoid leading prompts such as “Did the clear pricing help?” Do not ask participants to evaluate a feature before observing their use.

## Observation sheet

Use anonymous participant IDs P1–P5. Record task attempt, completion, assistance given, critical errors, first click, price comprehension, accommodation comprehension, ease rating, and exact participant language only with consent. Distinguish observed behavior, participant statements, researcher interpretation and unresolved questions.

The guided prototype captures local elapsed seconds, selected section/quantity/price, and actions. It separately records whether comparison was opened and a plan was copied or marked copied. These are instrumentation checks—not validated task-success measures. No data is sent to a server. Downloaded records remain on the device; the moderator must manage any research storage.

## Proposed decision criteria

Prioritize any failure to understand total cost or access limitations. Examine why each participant fails or succeeds; do not claim statistically reliable conversion or task-success improvements from a five-person sample. Use task time only with context, including think-aloud effects and assistance. If a comparison baseline is used, counterbalance task order and document the baseline accurately. Do not characterize the V1 sketch as SeatGeek's interface.

## Review and iteration

Group findings by severity and supporting evidence. Invite a designer, product manager, engineer and accessibility specialist to challenge interpretations when collaborators are available. Choose a small set of changes, update the decision log, and retest. None of these sessions have occurred yet.
